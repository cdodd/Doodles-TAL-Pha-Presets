Building Endless Highway from init

A pad's job is to fill space without demanding attention. This recipe builds the classic 80s pad, and the key decisions are about time: how a sound arrives, how it leaves, and how it keeps moving while it's held.

Setup

Choose Reset Parameters, then loop four-note chords with one chord per bar.

Each step's preview adds to the one before. Measurements compare it with the previous step.

  1. Set the level first

    • Volume5.0→to2.4

    Pads stack several notes, each adding level. At init volume, four-note chords would be much louder than a single note. So turn it down first to keep your ears and meters honest.

    Listen for: A plain, buzzy chord: static and organ-like.

  2. Slow the arrival

    • Env T1 (attack)0.0 (about 1 ms)→to5.0 (about 399 ms)
    • Env T3 (decay)0.0 (about 10 ms to -20 dB)→to6.0 (about 1.4 s to -20 dB)
    • Env L3 (sustain)10.0→to9.5

    An attack of about 400 ms lets chords swell in instead of being struck, so they feel like a bed rather than a part. It's still quick enough to land close to the beat.

    The other choice: Analog Dusk uses two stages instead, with a soft arrival on time and a slow bloom after it.

    Listen for: Chords now swell in, though they still stop abruptly.

    • Time to half level: 0 ms → 229 ms

  3. Slow the departure

    • Env T4 (release)0.0 (about 10 ms to -20 dB)→to5.5 (about 1.1 s to -20 dB)
    • Voices6→to8

    A long release lets chord changes overlap, which hides the join between them. Those overlapping notes use up voices, so go up to eight voices to prevent notes being stolen.

    Listen for: Chord changes melt into one another.

    • Tail after note-off: 20 ms → 600 ms

  4. Darken it

    • VCF Cutoff10.0 (about 21726 Hz at C4)→to6.2 (about 1284 Hz at C4)
    • VCF Key follow0.0→to4.0
    • VCF Env0.0→to0.8
    • VCF Res0.0→to0.5

    A pad shouldn't compete with the lead's top end. A cutoff around 1.4 kHz keeps it warm with some air. A very small filter envelope adds a little brightening as each chord arrives.

    Listen for: Warm and much less buzzy, sitting further back.

    • Brightness: 4,640 Hz → 1,055 Hz

  5. Give it internal motion

    • DCO PulseOff→toPulse PW
    • PWM0.0→to6.0
    • PWM Rate5.0→to4.5

    This is the first key decision. Adding a pulse whose width keeps changing means the harmonic balance never quite settles. The rate is slow, so it feels like breathing rather than an effect.

    The other choice: Aurora Haze gets its motion from a slow LFO on the filter instead, which moves brightness rather than texture.

    Listen for: The sound is now slowly shifting inside, even on a held chord.

    • Loudness: -28.5 LUFS → -20.7 LUFS

  6. Add a little weight

    • Sub level0.0 / 3→to0.5 / 3

    A quiet sub-octave adds some body. Keep it low, because the bass needs that space. Remember the panel's sub 'OFF' position is really a square, and the level is what controls it.

    Listen for: Slightly fuller, without getting heavy.

    • Energy below 150 Hz: 3% → 3%

  7. Add the chorus

    • ChorusOff→toOn
    • Chorus Rate5.0→to5.0

    This is the second key decision, and the sound of the 80s. A mono pad becomes wide and shimmering at the press of a button, as it did on so many original Roland synths.

    Listen for: A dramatic change: it now surrounds you.

    • Stereo width (side/mid): 0.00 → 0.59

  8. Finish it

    • MW to VCF0.0→to2.5
    • MW to DCO LFO1.0→to0.0
    • Reverb mixOFF→to-0.56
    • Reverb size8.0→to7.0

    A little reverb puts it in a room. Sending the wheel to the filter means you can lift the pad into a chorus by hand, and turning off the wheel's default vibrato avoids an unwanted wobble. That's the finished Endless Highway.

    Listen for: In this preview the wheel rises across the second half, and the pad opens up.

    • Wheel up vs wheel down brightness: 1.1x → 3.5x

What to take away

  • A held sound needs movement of its own. PWM and chorus provide it here, with no automation.
  • Stacking voices adds up quickly, so set the level first. This one needs far less volume than a single-note patch.
  • A long release needs spare voices: more release means more notes sounding at once.

Where to go next

Starting points for moving from Endless Highway to another patch in the bank:

  • Aurora HazeTry removing the pulse and adding VCF LFO 2.2 at about 0.18 Hz, with a sine wave.
  • Analog DuskLower Env L1 to 4.5 and use a slow Env T2 of 6.8, for a two-stage swell.
  • Ghost MallReplace PWM with a Square at Pulse Fine +12 cents, and add a slow DCO LFO for a tape wobble.
