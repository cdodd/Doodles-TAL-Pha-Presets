Building Minor Stab from init

Chord memory lets one key play a whole chord, and moving your finger moves it in parallel. That simple idea gave us a lot of early house and synth-pop. This recipe covers how to use it, and why the sound needs to be simple.

Setup

Choose Reset Parameters and loop a rhythmic line of single notes around A3. Don't play chords: the synth will do it.

Each step's preview adds to the one before. Measurements compare it with the previous step.

  1. Set the level first

    • Volume5.0→to5.0

    One key is about to play several notes, so set the level first. As it's a stab, it needs to be punchy with plenty of headroom.

    Listen for: A single buzzy saw line.

  2. Record the chord

    • Voices6→to6
    • Chord memoryNone→toC4 Eb4 G4

    This is the key decision. On the panel, press **REC** in the CHORD section, play C4, Eb4 and G4, then press it again. Each key you play now sounds a minor triad. Six voices leave room for release tails. In mono, only the top note would sound.

    The other choice: Try a minor 7th for a house stab (Club Neon), or a suspended 4th (Sus Hit), which has no third and fits over most keys.

    Listen for: Each note in the line is now a whole chord, moving in parallel.

    • Loudness: -23.5 LUFS → -18.6 LUFS

  3. Make it a stab

    • Env T3 (decay)0.0 (about 10 ms to -20 dB)→to4.0 (about 304 ms to -20 dB)
    • Env L3 (sustain)10.0→to0.0
    • Env T4 (release)0.0 (about 10 ms to -20 dB)→to3.0 (about 105 ms to -20 dB)

    With about 300 ms of decay and no sustain, every hit dies away, however long the key is held. That turns a chord into a rhythmic hit. The release keeps it from cutting off if notes are very short.

    Listen for: Now rhythmic, though still harsh.

    • Held level: -4 dB from peak → -61 dB from peak

  4. Close the filter

    • VCF Cutoff10.0 (about 21726 Hz at C4)→to4.2 (about 252 Hz at C4)
    • VCF Key follow0.0→to4.0

    Three saws at once have a lot of top end. Closing the filter leaves room for the envelope to do something with it.

    Listen for: Dull, for now.

    • Brightness: 4,140 Hz → 308 Hz

  5. Add the bark

    • VCF Env0.0→to3.6
    • VCF Res0.0→to1.2

    The envelope that's closing the amp now also closes the filter. A big, fast sweep gives each hit its bark, and a little resonance makes that sweep stand out.

    The other choice: Club Neon goes much further with resonance, for a more squelchy house stab.

    Listen for: A strong 'bark' on every hit: a real stab.

    • Brightness: 308 Hz → 1,277 Hz
    • Filter sweep: 0.0 octaves → 1.8 octaves

  6. Add width

    • ChorusOff→toOn
    • Chorus Rate5.0→to5.0

    Chorus widens the chord and adds a little movement, which helps it sound less like three identical oscillators.

    Listen for: Wider and softer around the edges.

    • Stereo width (side/mid): 0.00 → 0.57

  7. Finish it

    • MW to VCF0.0→to2.0
    • MW to DCO LFO1.0→to0.0
    • Reverb mixOFF→to-0.72

    A small amount of reverb, and a wheel for opening the filter into a brighter section. That's the finished Minor Stab.

    Listen for: In this preview the wheel rises across the second half, and the stabs open up.

    • Wheel up vs wheel down brightness: 1.0x → 2.8x

What to take away

  • Chord memory needs enough voices. In mono, only the top note sounds.
  • Notes are recorded relative to C4, so you can record any chord and play it from any key.
  • Parallel chords can fall outside your key, so choose your root notes by ear.

Where to go next

Starting points for moving from Minor Stab to another patch in the bank:

  • Sus HitTry recording C4, F4, G4 instead and adding a quiet square, plus a wide dotted delay.
  • Club NeonAdd Bb4 to the chord, raise resonance to 4.2, and darken the starting cutoff to 3.8.
  • Gated ChordRaise sustain to full and add a square LFO on the filter, synced to 1/8.
