Building Sunrise Fanfare from init

Synth brass is a saw with one very specific behaviour: the brightness rises just after the note starts. This recipe builds that bloom, and adds the velocity response that makes real brass sound alive.

Setup

Choose Reset Parameters, then loop short, punchy four-note chords.

Each step's preview adds to the one before. Measurements compare it with the previous step.

  1. Set the level first

    • Volume5.0→to4.8

    Stacked chords add up, so set a sensible level first. The saw stays: its full harmonic series is a good match for brass.

    Listen for: An open, buzzy chord: not yet brass.

  2. Close the filter

    • VCF Cutoff10.0 (about 21726 Hz at C4)→to5.0 (about 478 Hz at C4)
    • VCF Key follow0.0→to4.0
    • Voices6→to8

    Start from a closed-down tone so that the envelope has something to open. Key follow means higher notes in a chord stay bright, as higher brass does. More voices let stabs ring into one another.

    Listen for: Dark and muffled, as a starting point.

    • Brightness: 4,255 Hz → 403 Hz

  3. Open the filter with a delay

    • VCF Env0.0→to3.2
    • Env T1 (attack)0.0 (about 1 ms)→to2.5 (about 55 ms)

    This is the key decision. Filter and level now rise together over about 55 ms. That slight delay before the tone opens is what your ear hears as 'a section taking a breath and playing'. Sustain is still full, though, so the filter stays open.

    The other choice: With T1 at 3.8 you get Silk Horn's softer, slower speech. At 1.2 you get Stadium Stab's harder hit.

    Listen for: Each hit now blooms into brightness, and it's starting to sound like brass.

    • Time to half level: 0 ms → 25 ms

  4. Let it settle after the bloom

    • Env T3 (decay)0.0 (about 10 ms to -20 dB)→to4.5 (about 349 ms to -20 dB)
    • Env L3 (sustain)10.0→to6.5

    Brass is brightest as it speaks, then relaxes. With sustain below full, the filter and level both come back down after the bloom. On short stabs this gives extra punch.

    Listen for: Longer hits have a clear shape: they open and relax.

    • Held level: -1 dB from peak → -4 dB from peak

  5. Let velocity set the brightness

    • VCF Env modeNormal→toNormal-Dynamic

    Real brass gets brighter when it's played harder. Normal-Dynamic does the same with the filter, so a programmed part stops sounding flat.

    Listen for: Accented hits get noticeably brighter.

    • Hard note vs soft note brightness: 1.0x → 3.0x

  6. Make one synth a section

    • ChorusOff→toOn
    • Chorus Rate5.0→to5.0
    • VCF Res0.0→to0.5

    Chorus makes it sound like several players, slightly out of time with one another. A very small amount of resonance helps the bloom stand out.

    The other choice: Power Brass uses unison instead, which is much thicker but sounds more like a wall than a section.

    Listen for: Wider, like a whole section.

    • Stereo width (side/mid): 0.00 → 0.58

  7. Finish it

    • Env T4 (release)0.0 (about 10 ms to -20 dB)→to3.0 (about 105 ms to -20 dB)
    • MW to VCF0.0→to2.0
    • MW to DCO LFO1.0→to0.0
    • Reverb mixOFF→to-0.64
    • Reverb size8.0→to5.0

    A short release and a small plate put the section in a room, without blurring the stabs. The wheel can add some brightness for the last chorus. That's the finished Sunrise Fanfare.

    Listen for: In this preview the wheel rises across the second half, and it brightens further.

    • Tail after note-off: 32 ms → 317 ms
    • Wheel up vs wheel down brightness: 1.1x → 2.7x

What to take away

  • Brass isn't about the oscillator. It's the delayed filter bloom that turns a saw into a horn.
  • Envelope attack time affects both level and filter, so it sets how the brass speaks.
  • Velocity to filter adds the human response: real brass gets brighter when you play harder.

Where to go next

Starting points for moving from Sunrise Fanfare to another patch in the bank:

  • Stadium StabShorten T1 to 1.2, drop sustain to 1.2, and add unison and more reverb.
  • Silk HornSlow T1 to 3.8, close the filter to 4.2, and reduce VCF Env to 2.0.
  • Night PatrolPlay an octave down, close the filter to 3.4 and slow T1 to 4.2.
