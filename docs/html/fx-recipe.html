Building Riser from init

Most FX patches use a single control pushed well past where you'd normally set it. This recipe builds a riser, and shows how one long envelope can move both level and filter, so you only need to hold down one key.

Setup

Choose Reset Parameters and hold one long note. It doesn't matter much which: noise has no pitch.

Each step's preview adds to the one before. Measurements compare it with the previous step.

  1. Start from init

    A saw, wide open, with a sharp start. Nothing changes over time.

    Listen for: A constant buzz.

  2. Add noise

    • Noise0.0 / 3→to2.0 / 3

    Noise has no pitch, so a riser won't clash with your key. The saw is kept quietly underneath, so it's not just hiss.

    The other choice: Remove the saw entirely, as in Ocean Static, for pure air. Remove the noise for a purely tonal rise.

    Listen for: Airy and bright, still constant.

    • Brightness: 4,760 Hz → 10,599 Hz

  3. Close the filter almost entirely

    • VCF Cutoff10.0 (about 21726 Hz at C4)→to2.2 (about 51 Hz at C4)

    At about 60 Hz, only a dull rumble gets through. That's a far lower setting than you'd normally use, but it gives the sound a very long way to travel.

    Listen for: A low, muffled rumble.

    • Brightness: 10,599 Hz → 88 Hz

  4. Make the filter's journey long

    • VCF Env0.0→to6.8
    • Env T1 (attack)0.0 (about 1 ms)→to7.8 (about 4.2 s)
    • Env T3 (decay)0.0 (about 10 ms to -20 dB)→to5.0 (about 678 ms to -20 dB)
    • Env L3 (sustain)10.0→to10.0

    This is the key decision. An attack of about four seconds is well past normal, and a very large envelope amount takes the filter nearly all the way open. Since one envelope moves both level and filter, the sound gets both louder and brighter from a single held note.

    The other choice: Try lowering T1 to 6.5 for a rise of a little over a second. Rising Tide uses the same idea with strings instead of noise.

    Listen for: It rises by itself, from nothing to a bright rush.

    • Time to half level: 40 ms → 2819 ms

  5. Make the sweep audible

    • VCF Res0.0→to3.5

    A resonant peak lets you hear the filter moving through the noise. Without it, a noise sweep can sound like a simple fade. Don't overdo it, or it starts to whistle.

    Listen for: A slight whistle rising through the noise.

    • Brightness: 5,972 Hz → 6,989 Hz

  6. Let it ring out

    • Env T4 (release)0.0 (about 10 ms to -20 dB)→to3.5 (about 190 ms to -20 dB)
    • Voices6→to4
    • Reverb mixOFF→to-0.40
    • Reverb size8.0→to8.0

    A short release and a big reverb mean that when you let go on the downbeat, there's a tail that blends into the next section. A few voices are enough for occasional overlap.

    Listen for: A little more space and size around the rise.

    • Tail after note-off: 10 ms → 359 ms

  7. Set up the wheel

    • MW to VCF0.0→to2.0
    • MW to DCO LFO1.0→to0.0

    The wheel adds some brightness for a final push. The default vibrato is turned off, as it would only make the saw layer wobble.

    Listen for: In this preview the wheel rises across the second half, as an extra push at the end.

  8. Set the level

    • Volume5.0→to5.6

    The level is measured across the whole rise. That's the finished Riser.

    Listen for: The finished patch.

    • Loudness: -18.5 LUFS → -18.0 LUFS

What to take away

  • One long envelope moves level and filter at the same time, which is what a riser needs.
  • Noise suits FX because it has no pitch to clash with your key.
  • FX come from pushing a control to extremes. Try the same approach with pitch envelope or LFO depth.

Where to go next

Starting points for moving from Riser to another patch in the bank:

  • DownlifterReverse the idea: use a long decay with no sustain, and add DCO Env 7.8 for a falling pitch.
  • Ocean StaticTurn off the saw, set noise to full, and move the filter with a slow LFO rather than the envelope.
  • Night RainUse noise only, with HP 2, and a fast S&H LFO on the filter.
